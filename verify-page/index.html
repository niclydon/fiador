The founding attestation

Fiador's first published attestation binds the Discord application 1561422752521396254 (the bot Nova) to its operator's wallet registration. It is a signed JSON document: anyone can fetch it and verify, live, that the wallet it names is registered to a unique human, that the signature is the wallet's, and that the attestation has not expired.

Read the founding attestation (signed JSON, served from this site).

What it proves, and what it does not

A verified attestation proves a unique human backs the operator's wallet registration. It does not prove who that human is, that any particular message was written by a human, or that the software behind the bot behaves well. The attestation carries a hash of the human identifier, never the identifier itself.

The registry

Attestations are discovered through a small public registry: one entry per Discord application, pointing at the attestation's canonical URL. Listing is mechanical — if an attestation verifies and the entry is well-formed, it is listed.
