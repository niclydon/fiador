signed wallet claims about Discord bots

The founding claim

Fiador's first published record is a wallet-signed claim naming Discord application 1561422752521396254 (the bot Nova). Anyone can fetch the JSON document and check its signature, expiry, and the wallet's live AgentBook registration. Those checks do not establish that the signer controls the Discord application or bot.

Read the founding attestation (signed JSON, served from this site).

What the check establishes

A successful check establishes that a wallet registered to a unique human signed this statement. The Discord application ID and display name are claims supplied by the signer; application control has not been proven. The check does not identify the human, prove that a human wrote a bot message, or assess the bot's behavior. The record carries a hash of the human identifier, never the identifier itself.

The registry

Attestations are discovered through a small public registry: one entry per claimed Discord application, pointing at the record's canonical URL. Listing is mechanical; it does not endorse the claimed application owner or establish control of that app.

Read the registry (JSON; canonical copy lives in the public repository).
